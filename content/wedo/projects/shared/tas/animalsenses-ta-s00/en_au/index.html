Type of Project

This project is a design brief focusing on the development of computational thinking skills. While this project will activate all five of these skills, an emphasis can be placed on the skills that are most relevant to you and your students.

In this project, the focus could be:

  • Decomposition: simplifying the problem by identifying the different ways in which animals interact with their environment

  • Generalisation: finding patterns and relationships between animals’ senses and stimuli

  • Algorithmic Thinking: creating programs that use sensors

  • Evaluation: improving the model until it is as accurate as possible

  • Abstraction: explaining the solution with the right level of detail

Planning

Preparation: 30 Minutes

  • Select a specific theme to talk about with your students. For example, you could focus specifically on insects or reptiles.

  • Decide how you would like to introduce this project. Find some videos or information to read with your students.

  • Define the elements that your students should consider as they develop their models (e.g., using sensors to interact with the environment, different reactions according to various stimuli, etc.).

  • Determine the end result of this project. For example, define the parameters of your students’ presentations and the specific elements that they should include in their documents.

Explore Phase: 30–60 Minutes

Introduce your students to the subject and make sure that they understand the problem that they have to solve.

Create Phase: 45–60 Minutes (with the Test Phase)

Have your students build and program a model of an animal they have chosen. Make sure that they use sensors as an element in modelling the animal’s interaction with its environment.

Test Phase: 45–60 Minutes (with the Create Phase)

Let your students plan, try and modify their model until they get to a point where they are happy with what they have done. In order to challenge some of the more advanced teams, ask them to describe the environment in which the animal lives.

Share Phase: 45 Minutes or More

  • Make sure that your students document their work using the Documentation tool.

  • Use different ways in order to let your students share what they have learned and their reflections on these experiences.

  • Have your students create their final reports and present their projects.
