Type of Project

This project is a design brief focusing on the development of computational thinking skills. While this project will activate all five of these skills, an emphasis can be placed on the skills that are most relevant to you and your students.

In this project, the focus could be:

  • Decomposition: simplifying the problem by identifying a safety problem on city transportation

  • Generalization: finding patterns between different safety devices that can be used

  • Algorithmic Thinking: creating a program for each function in order to make city transportation safer

  • Evaluation: improving the solution until city transportation is safer

  • Abstraction: explaining the solution with the right level of detail

Planning

Preparation: 30 Minutes

  • Select a specific theme to talk about with your students. For example, you could focus specifically on truck safety in traffic situations.

  • Decide how you would like to introduce this project. Find some videos to watch or information to read with your students. You could also take a virtual or actual field trip around your neighborhood or city. Have students look for possible problems with current transportation (e.g., could a bus stop be made safer, could a traffic signal be improved, etc.).

  • Define the elements your students should consider as they develop their solutions (e.g., where and how do accidents happen, are there any statistics about these accidents, is there a safety device that could be improved, etc.).

  • Determine the end result of this project. For example, define the parameters of your students’ presentations and specific elements they should include in their documents. Be precise about whether you would like the students’ solutions to be physical (using LEGO® bricks), digital (using a program), or both.

Explore Phase: 30–60 Minutes

Introduce your students to the subject and make sure they understand the problem they have to solve.

Create Phase: 45–60 Minutes (with the Test Phase)

Have your students build and program a device that can improve city safety.

Test Phase: 45–60 Minutes (with the Create Phase)

Let your students plan, try, and modify their solutions until they get to a point where they are happy with what they have done. To challenge some of the more advanced teams, introduce some constraints or have them explore more than one solution.

Share Phase: 45 Minutes or More

  • Make sure your students document their work using the Documentation tool.

  • Use different ways to let students share what they have learned and their reflections on these experiences.

  • Have your students create their final reports and present their projects.
