Scientists around the world monitor the activity of various volcanos. In some cases, they do this in order to make sure that the people living around these volcanos are always safe. In other cases, they do it in order to prepare important scientific expeditions to study the composition of the earth.

Volcanos are described as being in one of three different stages: extinct, dormant, and active. For each of these stages, scientists have assigned a color, and have defined actions to be taken at each stage:

  • Green: the volcano is not showing any signs of activity.

  • Yellow: the volcano is showing some signs of activity and should be monitored.

  • Orange: the volcano is active with some minor emissions.

  • Red: the volcano is active with major emissions, or the volcano will erupt imminently.

Vocabulary Words

Volcanic Stages

The three different levels of activity that can describe the current state of a volcano (i.e., extinct, dormant, and active).

Eruption

In an active volcano, the state in which hot materials such as lava, rocks, dust, ash, and gas are ejected from the volcano.
