Type of Project

This project is a design brief focusing on the development of computational thinking skills. While this project will activate all five of these skills, an emphasis can be placed on the skills that are most relevant to you and your students.

In this project, the focus could be:

  • Decomposition: simplifying the problem by identifying the different actions a robot needs to perform while exploring narrow spaces

  • Generalization: finding patterns in the different ways a robot detects objects, holes, or other obstacles

  • Algorithmic Thinking: creating a program to move the robot within a maze

  • Evaluation: improving the solution until the robot can complete its task

  • Abstraction: explaining your solution with the right level of detail

Planning

Preparation: 30 Minutes

  • Select a specific theme to talk about with your students. For example, you could focus specifically on robots that inspect water pipes.

  • Decide how you would like to introduce this project. Find some videos to watch or information to read with your students.

  • Define the elements of the maze that the robots will go through (i.e., define holes, walls, obstacles to avoid or to collect, etc.).

  • Determine the end result of this project. For example, define the parameters of your students’ presentations and specific elements they should include in their documents.

Explore Phase: 30–60 Minutes

Introduce your students to the subject and make sure they understand the problem they have to solve.

Create Phase: 45–60 Minutes (with the Test Phase)

Have your students build and program a robot that can move in a maze. If they cannot create a robot that can turn, guide them to the Design Library for inspiration, or allow them to execute some movements with their hands.

Test Phase: 45–60 Minutes (with the Create Phase)

Let your students plan, try, and modify their solutions until they get to a point where they are happy with what they have done. To challenge some of the more advanced teams, introduce more obstacles or modify the maze to make it harder to navigate.

Share Phase: 45 Minutes or More

  • Make sure your students document their work using the Documentation tool.

  • Use different ways to let students share what they have learned and their reflections on these experiences.

  • Have your students create their final reports and present their projects.
