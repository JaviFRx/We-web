Computational Thinking Skills

Assessment does not have to encompass all of these skills, an emphasis can be placed on the skills that are most relevant to you and your students.

Enable Students to Find Patterns (Generalization)

A pattern is something that can be applied in the same way in different situations.

Here are some patterns your students should consider for this project:

  • How will the alarm device signal the stage of the volcano?

  • How can the Tilt Sensor be used in this model?

  • What movement(s) will be repeated?

Enable Students to Program a Sequence of Actions (Algorithmic Thinking)

The students will program their alarms to alert when the needle stops at the red position. Ask them to describe the steps they would like to program (e.g., when the alarm shakes, activate the motor, wait to see where it stops, give the appropriate signal according to the position of the device).

Enable Students to Improve Their Solution (Evaluation)

One of the challenges of this project is to understand the link between the alarm, the program, and the objects that must be moved. Have your students evaluate whether or not they think their solution meets all of these criteria:

  • The alarm is activated when it is shaken

  • The motor is activated for a random number of seconds

  • The Tilt Sensor is used to detect the stage of the volcano

  • Each time the red stage is reached, the drone is moved into the volcano

You can organize a sharing session between teams or in groups so that your students can exchange strategies or tips that could help them to improve their solutions.

Programming Help

Using the Tilt Sensor

The red stage of the alarm device is reached when the Tilt Sensor is tilted down.

Students can assign different types of alarms at that stage, such as sound, light, or images of an erupting volcano.
