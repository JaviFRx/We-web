Group Discussion Questions

  1. What are some different movements you can do with your hand?

    Students will probably describe movements with words such as “grabbing,” “holding,” and “throwing.” Have them describe in detail how the fingers are used in these motions.

  2. What is the role of muscles in your hand?

    Muscles are responsible for all of its movements.

  3. How can you create an artificial arm?

    A motor can be used to create movements similar to a human hand. It can also be used to apply pressure on objects in order to hold them.

Setting the Scene for This Project

This project requires the students to move small objects. Make sure there is enough room for them manipulate the hand and the objects. The distance between the flower and the table is not important, but this distance cannot be greater than one meter.

Computational Thinking Skills

Enabeling students to simplify the problem (Decomposition)

Decomposition is the main skill being developed during this phase. Therefore, the focus is on enabling students to decompose the problem.

In other words:

  • Is the student able to explain the problem by themselves?

  • Is the student able to describe how they will evaluate whether or not they were successful in solving the problem?

  • Is the student able to break down the problem into smaller and more manageable parts?

Suggested breakdown of this problem:

  • What movements will we try to replicate?

  • What objects need to be manipulated, and how?

  • In what order?

Assessing the Explore Phase

In the Explore phase, feedback should relate to whether or not the student is actively involved in the discussion by asking and answering questions, and their level of understanding of the problem.

Feedback can be given on a scale of 1 to 4:

  1. The student is unable to provide answers to questions or adequately participate in class discussions.

  2. With prompting, the student is able to provide answers to questions or adequately participate in class discussions.

  3. The student is able to provide adequate answers to questions and participate in class discussions.

  4. The student is able to provide extended explanations during class discussions.

Specific rubrics for assessing computational thinking skills can be found in the Teacher guide.
