Computational Thinking Skills

Use this task to evaluate whether or not your students have applied what they have learned in the previous task.

Setting the Scene

In response to the stage of the volcano, the students will need to move the objects to new locations following these indications:

  • The drone is flying in the air but will only go into the volcano when the alarm is red. This is so it can explore the erupting volcano.

  • The animal that is normally close to the volcano will move away toward the river when the volcano shows signs of activity, and even farther away when the volcano is erupting.

  • When the alarm is green, the rover will wait at the scientist’s house. When the alarm is yellow, the rover will go to the same location as the animal. When the alarm is red, it will climb up the volcano to collect rock samples.

Programming Help

Assigning Tasks to Three Sensor Modes

The Tilt Sensor in this model can detect three different modes:

  • Tilt Up will point the needle to green,

  • No Tilt will point the needle to yellow,

  • Tilt Down will point the needle to red.

To assign different tasks to these three modes, students can use the Send and Receive message blocks.

Actions placed after the Wait For Block will be specific to that mode.

This program introduces the concept of condition. The send message "xyz" activates three strings at the same time. Only one of the Wait For Block can be triggered at a time, depending on the mode of the tilt sensor.

Resetting the Display

To remove the image from the display, use image "0" (the white image) in your programming string.

Opportunities for Differentiation

This project can be made more challenging by adding one or more of the following requirements:

  • Have the students create flowcharts that correctly show how each object should behave according to each situation.

  • Have the students create other objects and other conditional statements.

Assessing the Test Phase

During the Test phase, make sure the student works well on a team, justifies his or her best solution, and uses the information collected during the Explore phase.

Feedback can be given on a scale of 1 to 4:

  1. The student is unable to work well on a team, justify solutions, and use information collected for further development.

  2. The student is able to work on a team, collect and use information with guidance, or with help, to justify solutions.

  3. The student is able to work on a team and contribute to the team discussions, justify solutions, and collect and use information about the content.

  4. The student can justify and discuss solutions that allow for the collection and use of information.

Specific rubrics for assessing computational thinking skills can be found in the Teacher guide.
