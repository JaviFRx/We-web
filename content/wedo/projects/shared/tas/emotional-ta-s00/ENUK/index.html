Type of Project

This project is a design brief focusing on the development of computational thinking skills. While this project will activate all five of these skills, an emphasis can be placed on the skills that are most relevant to you and your pupils.

In this project, the focus could be:

  • Decomposition: simplifying the problem by identifying different ways in which robots can interact with people

  • Generalisation: finding patterns and relationships between motors or sensors and actions that generate emotions

  • Algorithmic Thinking: creating programs that can be linked to robot behaviour

  • Evaluation: improving the robot’s interactions in order to generate emotions

  • Abstraction: explaining the solution with the right level of detail

Planning

Preparation: 30 Minutes

  • Select a specific theme to talk about with your pupils. For example, you could focus specifically on robots that can make people laugh.

  • Decide how you would like to introduce this project. Find some videos to watch or information to read with your pupils.

  • Define the elements that your pupils should focus on (e.g., movement, sensors, expressions, etc.).

  • Determine the end result of this project. For example, define the parameters of your pupils’ presentations and the specific elements that they should include in their documents.

Explore Phase: 30–60 Minutes

Introduce your pupils to the subject and make sure that they understand the problem that they have to solve.

Create Phase: 45–60 Minutes (with the Test Phase)

Have your pupils build and program a robot that can make people laugh. Have them program specific behaviours for their robot.

Test Phase: 45–60 Minutes (with the Create Phase)

Let your pupils plan, try and modify their solutions until they get to a point where they are happy with what they have done. In order to challenge some of the more advanced teams, introduce new parameters such as generating different emotions.

Share Phase: 45 Minutes or More

  • Make sure that your pupils document their work using the Documentation tool.

  • Use different ways in order to let your pupils share what they have learned and their reflections on these experiences.

  • Have your pupils create their final reports and present their projects.
